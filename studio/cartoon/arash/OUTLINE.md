# Arash the Archer · آرش کمانگیر — 3 parts

Sources: Abu Rayhan al-Biruni, al-Athar al-Baqiya (the festival of Tirgan): Afrasiab besieged Manuchehr in
Tabaristan; they agreed that Iran's border would be where an arrow shot by an Iranian landed; the angel Spandarmad
told how the bow and arrow must be made; Arash shot it, gave his life with the shot, and the arrow flew to the far
edge of Khorasan (Biruni: a walnut tree; later tellings: the bank of the Oxus/Jeyhun). Encyclopaedia Iranica,
"ĀRAŠ" (A. Tafazzoli). A short legend → 3 parts. Siavash Kasraei's 1959 poem is modern and under copyright: do not
use its lines or its invented details; tell the old legend.

Tone: epic and quiet. Arash's death is not shown as violence: his body gives all its strength to the arrow; he
fades into light and wind at the top of the mountain.

## Characters

| role | look | voice |
|---|---|---|
| NARRATOR | — | series narrator |
| ARASH | a lean archer of about 30, plain wool clothes, leather bracer, long dark hair tied back, calm face; a huge bow | calm, warm baritone, few words |
| MANUCHEHR | king of Iran, tired by the long siege, gold crown, blue and gold robe | dignified, worried |
| AFRASIAB | king of Turan, sharp helmet, black and red armour, smiling | arrogant, clipped |
| SPANDARMAD | guardian angel of the earth: a woman of soft green light | gentle, echoing |
| ARASH'S MOTHER (or wife) | village woman, blue scarf | soft |
| SOLDIERS, people of both sides, two Turanian witnesses | as needed | — |

Places: a besieged hill fortress in green Tabaristan with tents of Turan all around; hungry streets; the king's
tent; a forest at night (wood for the bow), an eagle's nest (feathers), a forge (arrowhead); the snowy summit of a
great mountain at dawn (Damavand, as the popular telling has it) with people of both armies below; the arrow's
flight over deserts and rivers; a walnut tree on the bank of a wide river; a Tirgan water festival today.

## Parts

| part | title | what happens |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The Siege · محاصره | Hook: an arrow flies over mountains at sunrise; narrator: "This is the arrow that drew a border." Years of war; Afrasiab's army surrounds King Manuchehr in Tabaristan; hunger in the fortress. Both sides are tired; Afrasiab mocks Manuchehr's offer of peace, then agrees: Iran keeps every land as far as one arrow can fly. He laughs: how far can an arrow go? Turn: Manuchehr looks at his men: "Who can shoot that arrow?" Silence; one man steps forward: Arash. |
| 2 | The Bow · کمان | Spandarmad appears to Manuchehr at night and tells him how the bow and arrow must be made: wood from a certain forest, feathers of an eagle, a point of fine iron. Craftsmen make them. Arash is the best archer, a quiet man; he talks with the king (why he will do it) and says goodbye to his mother. Before dawn he climbs the mountain; people of both armies wait below; two Turanian witnesses watch. Turn: at the top Arash takes off his coat and shows his body to everyone: "Look at me. I am whole and strong. After this shot, I will not be." |
| 3 | Arash's Arrow · تیر آرش | Sunrise. Arash draws the bow with all his strength; the arrow flies. His body gives all its life to the shot and he becomes light and wind on the summit (no violence). The arrow flies all morning, over mountains, deserts and rivers, until it strikes a walnut tree on the far bank of a great river. The witnesses ride back; Afrasiab keeps his word; that river is the border. Ending: Tirgan, the 13th of the month Tir: people still splash water and tie coloured threads; narrator: "Some say the arrow is still flying." |
