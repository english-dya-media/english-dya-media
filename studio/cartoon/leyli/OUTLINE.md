# Leyli & Majnun · لیلی و مجنون — 8 parts

Source: Nizami Ganjavi, Leyli o Majnun (1188), in the Khamsa, on Ganjoor: https://ganjoor.net/nezami/5ganj/leyli-majnoon/
(the story itself runs from the school to the deaths and Zayd's dream; skip the opening praises). About 4,500
couplets, but the events are few and slow → 8 parts. Dialogue is ours and follows the poem's events.

Tone: tender and sad, told simply. Deaths are quiet (a lamp goes out, an empty bed, flowers on a grave). Majnun's
"madness" is love, never mocked; keep him gentle. The desert animals (gazelles, a deer, a lion, birds) are his family.

## Characters

| role | look | voice |
|---|---|---|
| NARRATOR | — | series narrator |
| QAYS / MAJNUN | a boy of 12 (part 1), then a thin young man, black curly hair growing long and wild, torn white shirt, bare feet in the desert | soft, poetic young male |
| LEYLI | a girl of 12, then a young woman: black braids, dark eyes, red and indigo dress, silver jewellery, a veil outdoors | clear, gentle female |
| MAJNUN'S FATHER | chief of the Banu Amir, grey beard, white headdress, kind and sad | old, loving |
| LEYLI'S FATHER | stern chief of Leyli's tribe, black beard, black headdress | hard, proud |
| LEYLI'S MOTHER | kind, worried, dark veil | soft |
| TEACHER | old teacher of the tent school | dry, funny |
| NAWFAL | brave Arab chief, armour, lance, big horse | loud, generous |
| IBN SALAM | rich young man, fine silk, gold rings; patient, not a villain | polite |
| OLD MESSENGER | old man who carries the letters | creaky |
| ZAYD | Majnun's friend (end) | warm |
| hunter, tribesmen, warriors | as needed | — |

Places: a tent school in the desert; palm groves and tents of two tribes; the Kaaba courtyard (shown simply, from
outside, with respect); the open desert at day and night with stars; a hunter's trap; a battle in the sand (dust,
lances, no blood); Leyli's tent and garden in autumn; a grave under a palm tree.

## Parts

| part | title | what happens |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The School · مکتب | Hook: a thin man in rags writes a name in the sand while gazelles watch; the wind erases it; he writes it again: Leyli. Then years earlier: Qays, son of the chief of the Banu Amir, and Leyli at the same school; they look at each other more than at their lessons; the children whisper; the teacher sighs. Leyli's family hear the talk and keep her at home. Turn: Qays walks the streets calling her name; the boys shout "Majnun!" (madman), and the name stays. |
| 2 | The Proposal · خواستگاری | Majnun's father goes with gifts to ask for Leyli for his son. Leyli's father: "Your son is mad. Cure him, then we'll talk." Refused. Majnun goes to the desert. His father takes him to the Kaaba to pray to be free of this love; Majnun prays to love her more ("take my life, give it to Leyli"). Turn: Leyli's tribe ask the ruler for permission to kill Majnun; his father runs to warn him. |
| 3 | Nawfal · نوفل | Nawfal, a brave chief, finds Majnun in the desert, hears his story and swears to win Leyli for him. He sends a demand, then fights Leyli's tribe twice (dust, lances, shouting). In the battle Majnun prays for Leyli's side. Nawfal wins, but Leyli's father comes in the dust: "Kill her if you want; I will not give her to a madman." Nawfal gives up. Turn: Leyli's father promises her to Ibn Salam, a rich young man. |
| 4 | Leyli's Wedding · عروسی لیلی | Leyli is married to Ibn Salam; she tells him she will never be his wife in truth and he must stay away; he agrees, hoping she will change. Leyli secretly cries at night. In the desert a traveller tells Majnun the news; Majnun answers him with a poem and goes deeper into the wilderness. Turn: an old man walks into the desert with a stick: Majnun's father. |
| 5 | The Father · پدر | Father and son meet; the father begs him to come home and live; Majnun can't. The father goes home and dies (an empty seat at the tent; a funeral far away); Majnun weeps on his grave. Back in the desert Majnun buys a gazelle from a hunter and frees it, because its eyes are like Leyli's; he lives with the animals. Turn: an old messenger comes through the dunes: "I bring a letter from Leyli." |
| 6 | The Letters · نامه‌ها | Leyli's letter (her voice over the desert) and Majnun's answer (his voice over her garden). The old messenger arranges a meeting in a palm grove; Leyli comes and stops ten steps away; she cannot come nearer, he cannot either; he recites love verses; she leaves in tears. Salam of Baghdad, a young poet, visits Majnun and listens. Turn: a messenger runs to Leyli's tent: Ibn Salam is dead. |
| 7 | Autumn · پاییز | Leyli is a widow; now she can cry openly, and everyone thinks it is for her husband. Autumn in the garden; she grows weak and pale. She tells her mother her last wish: "When he comes, tell him I kept my love to the end." Turn: the lamp in Leyli's tent goes out (cut to black). |
| 8 | At Her Grave · کنار مزار | Majnun hears the news and comes to her grave with his animals. He talks to her, lies down by the grave and does not rise again (quiet; the animals keep watch). His friends bury him beside Leyli. Ending: Zayd dreams of them together in a garden of light; narrator closes: love outlived them. |
