# Mah Pishani · ماه‌پیشونی — 5 parts

Source: the Iranian folk tale of the girl with the moon on her forehead, told in many versions; an old English
record is "The Story of Little Fatima" in D. L. R. and E. O. Lorimer, Persian Tales (1919): the teacher who wants to
marry the father, the mother who turns into a yellow cow after falling into the vinegar jar, the stepmother's
cotton, the div at the bottom of the well who blesses the polite girl with a moon on her forehead and a star on her
chin, the shoe, and the search. This telling is ours; don't copy modern retellings (books, films, the names they
invented). Medium-length tale → 5 parts.

Tone: a fairy tale with a brave, kind girl. The mother's fall into the jar is not shown (the girl only does what
the teacher told her, not knowing; a lid, a splash sound, silence; forty days later a yellow cow comes out with her
mother's eyes). The cow is taken away, not killed on screen. The stepsister's "punishment" is comic (a donkey's tail
on her forehead that wags when she lies).

## Characters

| role | look | voice |
|---|---|---|
| NARRATOR | — | series narrator, warm |
| THE GIRL / MAH PISHANI | a girl of 12, then 16: dark braids, kind eyes, patched blue dress; after part 3 a silver crescent moon shining on her forehead and a small star on her chin, hidden under a scarf | sweet, brave female |
| FATHER | a quiet weaver, weak-willed, short beard | soft male |
| MOTHER | gentle, brown dress (part 1); then the YELLOW COW with her mother's eyes, who can talk only to the girl | warm female |
| MOLLA BAJI | the girls' teacher, then the stepmother: sweet smile, sharp eyes, many bracelets, purple scarf | honey-sweet, then sharp |
| STEPSISTER | the teacher's daughter: spoiled, rude, pouting; after part 4 a donkey's tail on her forehead | whiny |
| DIV | an old she-div at the bottom of the well: huge, wild grey hair, long nails, one tooth; grumpy but fair | deep, creaky |
| PRINCE | the king's son, kind, green and gold | young male |
| PRINCE'S MOTHER | the queen, who searches with the shoe | dignified |
| ROOSTER | the family rooster who tells the truth | crowing voice |

Places: a girls' maktab room; the family house with a big vinegar jar in the storeroom; a meadow and stream where
the girl spins cotton beside the cow; a deep well that opens into the div's messy underground house; the bath house
(where the women see the moon); a royal festival; a stream with stepping stones; the kitchen with the clay oven
(tanoor); a royal wedding.

## Parts

| part | title | what happens |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The Teacher · ملاباجی | Hook: a girl climbs out of a well at night; light shines from her forehead. Then: the girl at school; the teacher spoils her with sweets and says: "If I were your mother, I'd give you honey every day." She tells her a "game": ask your mother for vinegar from the big jar, then close the lid. The girl does it, not understanding; the lid, a splash, silence (nothing shown). Her father, lonely, marries the teacher. Turn: forty days later a yellow cow walks out of the vinegar jar and looks at the girl with her mother's eyes. |
| 2 | The Yellow Cow · گاو زرد | The stepmother has her own daughter now and treats the girl like a servant. Every day: take the cow to the meadow and spin a mountain of cotton. The cow says, "Put the cotton in my mouth," and pulls out fine thread. The stepmother is suspicious. One day the wind blows a ball of cotton into a deep well. Turn: the girl climbs down the well and finds a door at the bottom: a div's house. |
| 3 | The Div in the Well · دیو چاه | The cow's advice: "Be polite, and do the opposite of what she says." The old div: "Knock down my house!" The girl sweeps and tidies it. "Is my hair full of lice?" "No, it's full of pearls." The div, pleased, gives back the cotton and sends her up the well: when the yellow water comes, don't touch it; when the white water comes, wash your face. She does; a moon shines on her forehead and a star on her chin. Turn: at home the stepmother pulls off her scarf: "Where did you get that?!" |
| 4 | The Stepsister · دختر نامادری | The stepmother sends her own daughter down the well; she is rude, breaks the div's things, insults her hair; she comes back with a donkey's tail on her forehead (comic). Furious, the stepmother pretends to be ill: "Only the yellow cow's meat can cure me." The cow tells the girl: "Don't be sad. Don't eat my meat; keep my bones in a jar and bury them." The cow is led away (not shown). Turn: a royal festival; the stepmother takes her daughter and leaves the girl with an impossible job: mix of rice and lentils to sort before night. |
| 5 | The Shoe · لنگه‌ی کفش | A hen and chicks sort the grains (or the cow's spirit helps); the buried jar holds a beautiful dress and golden shoes. She goes to the festival, shines like the moon, and runs home before her stepmother; crossing a stream she loses one shoe. The prince finds it; the queen goes from house to house trying it on every girl. The stepmother hides the girl in the cold oven and pushes her daughter forward (the tail wags). The rooster crows: "The moon is in the oven!" They find her; the shoe fits. Ending: a wedding; the stepmother and stepsister walk away in shame. |
