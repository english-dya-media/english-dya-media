# Zahhak & Fereydun · ضحاک و فریدون — 10 parts

Source: Ferdowsi's Shahnameh, end of «جمشید» (sections 2–4: Mardas and Zahhak, the snakes, the fall of Jamshid) and
«ضحاک» (sections 1–12), on Ganjoor: https://ganjoor.net/ferdousi/shahname/jamshid/ and
https://ganjoor.net/ferdousi/shahname/zahak/ (sh1 … sh12). About 550 couplets, dense with events → 10 parts.
Dialogue is ours (dramatised) and must follow what happens in the poem; no invented events.

Tone: a dark fairy tale told for learners. The snakes, the brains and the deaths are **never shown**: say it in a
line, show shadows, a closing door, a frightened face. Zahhak's snakes are two black cartoon snakes rising from his
shoulders (new prop in `chars.py`), menacing but not gory.

Places: Mardas's palace garden at dawn (a pit by the path), royal kitchen, Zahhak's dark throne hall (black and gold,
torches), Jamshid's throne hall, a meadow with the colourful cow, Mount Alborz with a hermit's cave, a blacksmith's
forge, a city square, the Arvand river (Tigris) with boats, Zahhak's palace (Gang Dezhhukht), Mount Damavand with a
cave. Banners: Zahhak's black serpent banner; from part 7 Kaveh's leather apron on a spear (Derafsh-e Kaviani,
purple, red and gold silk with jewels after part 8).

## Parts

| part | title | what happens |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The Prince and the Stranger · شاهزاده و غریبه | Hook: a dark throne at night, a king with two snakes on his shoulders whispering; narrator: "how does a good king's son become this?" Mardas, a kind king of the plains whose herds give milk to everyone. His son Zahhak is brave but light-headed. Iblis, as a smiling stranger, wins his trust and makes him swear an oath, then tells him the throne should be his now. Zahhak refuses at first, then lets Iblis dig a pit on his father's path in the garden. Turn: before dawn Mardas walks into the dark garden (cut away; we never see the fall). |
| 2 | The King's Cook · آشپز شاه | Zahhak is king. Iblis comes back as a cook; until then people ate only plants. Each day a richer meal: egg yolks, then partridge and pheasant, then chicken and lamb, then veal with saffron and rose water. Zahhak, delighted: "Ask for anything." The cook asks only to kiss the king's shoulders. He kisses them and vanishes. Turn: two black snakes rise from Zahhak's shoulders. |
| 3 | The Doctor · پزشک | The snakes are cut off; they grow back like branches. Doctors fail. Iblis comes as a doctor: "Feed them the brains of two young men every day; maybe they will die." (Said, never shown.) Meanwhile in Iran, Jamshid, after centuries of glory, says "I made the world"; his divine glory (farr) leaves him and his nobles turn away. They ride to Zahhak and make him king of Iran. Turn: Zahhak sits on Jamshid's throne; Jamshid runs; his sisters Arnavaz and Shahrnaz are brought to the palace. |
| 4 | The Two Cooks · دو آشپز | A thousand years of fear (narrator). Every night two young men are taken to the palace kitchen. Armayel and Garmayel, two good men, learn to cook and get into the kitchen. Each night they save one of the two, use a sheep's brain instead, and secretly send the young man away to the mountains with a few goats. Faces of mothers waiting at night. Turn: Zahhak wakes screaming from a dream; Arnavaz: "What did you see?" |
| 5 | Zahhak's Dream · خواب ضحاک | The dream: three warriors come out of the palace, the youngest strikes Zahhak with an ox-headed mace, ties him and drags him to Mount Damavand. Arnavaz tells him to call the wise men. The mobeds are afraid to speak for days; Zirak finally says: a boy not yet born, Fereydun, will do it, to avenge his father and a cow that will feed him. Zahhak's men search the land. Turn: in a small house a baby is born, Fereydun, and his father Abtin is caught on the road by Zahhak's men (cut away). |
| 6 | The Cow Barmayeh · گاو برمایه | Faranak, alone with the baby, runs to a meadow where a keeper guards Barmayeh, a cow with a coat of many colours. She begs him to raise the boy; the cow feeds him for three years. Zahhak hears of the cow. Faranak takes the boy up Mount Alborz to a holy man. Zahhak's soldiers reach the meadow; the cow and the keeper are gone (dust, broken fence; nothing shown). Turn: sixteen years later a tall young man comes down from Alborz: "Mother, who was my father?" |
| 7 | Kaveh the Blacksmith · کاوه‌ی آهنگر | Faranak tells Fereydun everything; he wants to fight now; she asks him to wait. In the palace, Zahhak, afraid of the name Fereydun, makes the nobles sign a paper saying he is a just king. Kaveh the blacksmith bursts in shouting: eighteen sons, seventeen taken for the snakes, and now the last. Zahhak gives back his son and asks him to sign. Kaveh reads it, tears it to pieces and walks out. Turn: in the market he ties his leather apron to a spear and raises it: "Who wants to be free, follow me!" |
| 8 | The Blacksmith's Banner · درفش کاویانی | The crowd follows Kaveh to Fereydun; Fereydun honours the apron and dresses it with silk and jewels: the Kaviani banner. He draws the ox-headed mace in the dust; the blacksmiths forge it. The march begins. At night his two older brothers (Kayanush and Purmayeh), jealous, roll a boulder down a hill at his tent; Fereydun wakes and stops it with a word. Sorush, a figure of light, visits him. Turn: at the Arvand river the ferryman refuses boats without Zahhak's seal. |
| 9 | Crossing the Arvand · گذر از اروند | Fereydun rides his horse straight into the river; the army follows, horses swimming. They reach Zahhak's palace while Zahhak is away in India. Fereydun breaks the magic, sits on the throne and frees Arnavaz and Shahrnaz ("I am the son of Abtin"). Kondrow the steward bows to the new king, then slips out at night. Turn: Kondrow kneels before Zahhak: "A young man with an ox-headed mace sits on your throne." |
| 10 | Damavand · دماوند | Zahhak comes back in secret at night and climbs into the palace by a rope, jealous and furious. Fereydun meets him and strikes with the mace (the helmet rings; Zahhak falls). Sorush stops the second blow: "Not yet. Bind him and take him to Damavand." Zahhak is chained in a cave deep in the mountain. Fereydun is crowned on the first day of Mehr; people light fires: Mehregan. Ending: Kaveh's banner over a free land. |
